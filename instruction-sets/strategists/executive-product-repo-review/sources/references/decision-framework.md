# Business decision framework

## Assess each meaningful feature
Assess these dimensions qualitatively: relevance to primary job; user frequency and urgency; time-to-first-value; differentiation; retention contribution; monetization/organizational return; usability/trust; complexity and maintenance burden; dependency risk; evidence strength. Use High/Medium/Low or Unknown; do not pretend precision.

**Protect:** mission-critical feature or substantiated advantage. **Improve:** user-value feature with high friction/defects/poor discoverability. **Test:** promising but unvalidated bet; define cheapest meaningful experiment. **Defer:** valuable later, not needed for MVP proof. **Cut:** low value, overlapping, high drag or confusing; state risk of removal and whether migration is needed.

## Decision principles
- MVP is the minimum coherent, lovable *end-to-end outcome*, not the fewest screens.
- The strongest feature is the one delivering differentiated customer value, not necessarily the most technologically impressive.
- Consider must-have parity separately from advantage-making differentiation.
- Feature usage ≠ feature value; return visits ≠ healthy retention. Measure meaningful repeat outcomes and cohorts.
- Retention mechanisms must emerge from recurring user needs, not manipulative engagement tactics.
- Discuss willingness to pay and packaging only as hypotheses without price/customer data.
- Differentiate technical gaps (missing capability), experience gaps (workflow difficulty), strategy gaps (unclear positioning), and evidence gaps (unknown demand).
- Consider first-time and returning users, edge cases, accessibility, support, privacy and reliability where material.

## 3 horizons
**Now / 0–3 months (illustrative):** sharpen value proposition, close the first-value journey, remove distractions, instrument outcomes and validate demand.
**Next / 3–9 months (illustrative):** repeat-value loop, strengthen clear differentiation, improve onboarding and retention.
**Later / 9–24+ months (illustrative):** scalable platforms, network effects, adjacent segments and strategic expansion after clear gates.
Time bands are planning heuristics, not delivery commitments; adjust to team/resources. Explicitly explain why ambitious future bets should not pollute MVP.

## Outcome/experiment pairing
For every major recommendation name: who benefits; pain solved; expected behavior change; smallest evidence-gathering step; leading signal (activation/time-to-value/return-on-core-job/task completion); guardrail (trust/support burden/cost); stop/pivot/scale criterion. When no telemetry is available, recommend what to measure rather than inventing baselines.

## Balanced opposition
Each strategic bet should show: best-case upside; major downside and opportunity cost; key uncertainty; reversible test or decision gate.
